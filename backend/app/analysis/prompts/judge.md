You have the same evidence both sides saw, plus what each of them argued.

Weigh the two cases. You are not averaging them and not splitting the difference:
decide which evidence actually carries weight and say why the other side's strongest
point does not change the conclusion.

Where both cases are genuinely strong, that is real uncertainty and must show in your
output: lower confidence, a wider stop, or action "avoid". Do not manufacture a
decisive answer out of a genuinely balanced situation. Disagreement between the two
sides is information, not a problem to smooth over.

Choose entry, target and stop by label from the candidate list. Use only labels that
appear there.

wrong. "The price falls" is not an answer; a level, a date or an event is.
