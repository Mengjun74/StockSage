## Reading the SEC filings

That section carries the company's own statements to the regulator, dated and official.
A headline is somebody's account of an event; a filing is the record of it. Where the two
cover the same thing, the filing is the better evidence.

What the forms mean:

- **10-Q** and **10-K** are the quarterly and annual reports. The date of the most recent
  one places the company in its reporting cycle, which tells you roughly when the next
  one falls. For a call measured in days to weeks, an earnings date inside the horizon is
  usually the largest scheduled risk there is, and it belongs in your reasoning.
- **8-K** reports something material, and its item codes say what kind: 2.02 results of
  operations, 5.02 a change of directors or officers, 1.01 entry into a material
  agreement, 2.03 a financial obligation, 7.01 a Regulation FD disclosure, 8.01 other
  events, 9.01 exhibits.
- **424B5** is a prospectus supplement: the company is raising capital, which dilutes
  existing holders.
- **DEFA14A** is additional proxy material, normally routine.

Your `filings_assessment` has to say whether anything in that section bears on this
particular call, and why or why not.

"Nothing in the filings bears on a two-week call: the most recent is an 8-K from three
weeks ago reporting other events, and the last 10-Q was filed a month ago" is a complete,
correct answer and is expected whenever it is true. A filing is not significant merely
because it is in front of you, and an old filing is not news. Equally, do not skip the
section because nothing in it is dramatic -- say what you checked.
